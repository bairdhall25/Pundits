# College football Week 1 results: 12 underdog picks, one winner

Pundits.Pro's tracked college football picks finished Week 1 of the 2026 season at **28–11 on straight-up winners**. The split: **27–0 on favorites, 1–11 on underdogs**, using our dated Kalshi snapshots to classify each selection.

The exception was Chip Patterson's call for Tulsa to beat Oklahoma State. Our dated Kalshi snapshot put Tulsa at 18¢. The Golden Hurricane won 24–10.

Those numbers describe 39 selections from 17 pundits across 11 games. Several people picked the same teams, so 12 underdog picks meant seven different underdogs. Only Tulsa won. These are winner-pick results, not records against the spread, and they cover the predictions we tracked rather than every pick those pundits made.

The underdogs ranged from FIU at 17¢ to California at 48¢ in our snapshots. California drew three picks, more than any other underdog in the sample. Clemson, FIU and Florida State each drew two.

## Who picked Tulsa to upset Oklahoma State?

Patterson picked Tulsa on Cover 3 before the game. His preserved call ended: “I'm gonna go Tulsa plus 450.” Tulsa then delivered the only winning underdog selection in our Week 1 sample. [Original receipt and result](https://pundits.pro/picks/oklahoma-state-at-tulsa-2026/patterson/)

His other tracked underdog call was Toledo, which lost 30–20 at Michigan State. Patterson therefore finished 1–1 on his two Week 1 underdog picks. The Tulsa hit stands alongside the Toledo miss in his record. [Toledo receipt](https://pundits.pro/picks/toledo-at-michigan-state-2026/patterson/)

## How did favorites perform in Week 1?

Our 27 winning favorite selections covered eight different teams in eight games. LSU alone accounted for ten winning picks; Notre Dame supplied seven more. Those two results produced 17 of the 27 hits.

Across all 11 games we covered, the favorite in our dated snapshots went 10–1. Oklahoma State was the losing favorite, but nobody in our ledger had picked it. South Florida and Michigan State also had no tracked favorite-side selections, despite winning their games.

That is why a pundit-pick record and a game-level record answer different questions. The 27–0 figure describes the favorite selections we captured. It does not mean 27 separate favorites won, or that our ledger contains every prediction those pundits made.

## What does this tell us about expert pick accuracy?

A winning percentage becomes more informative when you can see the selections underneath it: favorites or underdogs, how many distinct games, and which public calls produced the record. One week of this selected sample cannot establish who has a lasting advantage at predicting upsets.

## How we counted the picks

We used the published game-winner picks for college football Week 1 of the 2026 season, checked September 10. All 39 were graded. Each pundit counts once per game. Week 0, NFL, season-long predictions, tentative commentary and takes without a specific tracked event and side are excluded.

For this report, underdogs are picked sides below 50¢ in the displayed dated Kalshi snapshot; favorites are above 50¢. No selection was missing a price or exactly 50¢. These shared event snapshots are not closing odds or necessarily the prices when a prediction was made. The records measure straight-up results, not spread covers or betting returns.

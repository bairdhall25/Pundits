# StreetCred Competitive Profile

Status: Evidence with an active monitoring checklist

Baseline researched: 2026-08-29

Website: https://howrightyouare.substack.com/

Builder: Steve Wood (SwoodWorks)

## Current assessment

StreetCred is an editorial accountability ledger on Substack. The thesis overlaps Pundits ("named people made claims; the record should exist"). The object does not: essays about draft boards, finals, and World Cup predictions, not a weekly CFB/NFL pick product with cards and grades at event URLs.

Low commercial threat. Useful as a reminder that "pundit ledger" is a crowded phrase in 2026 and that Pundits wins by being a sports fan product, not a manifesto.

## Baseline signals

- Launch post on swoodworks.com describes StreetCred as a pundit accountability ledger that captures public predictions from named media personalities and matches them to outcomes. Tagline: How Right You Are.
- Substack on 2026-08-29 showed Issue 22 within the last day, plus June 2026 posts on NHL draft boards, Sam Vecenie NBA draft portraits, NBA Finals, and a World Cup prediction ledger.
- LinkedIn (Steve Wood, May 2026) described starting with sports because the feedback loop is fast, with AI assistance, and pointed people to the Substack.
- No official X handle was verified in this pass.
- No app, API, frozen prices, or weekly CFB/NFL slate was verified.
- Traction (subscriber counts, revenue) was not verified.

## Competitive interpretation

### Strengths

- Clear thesis and patient editorial cadence.
- Willing to grade slow events (drafts, finals) where Pundits' current scope is parked or thin.
- Positions the reader, not the pundit, as the customer ("you gave them your attention").

### Weaknesses

- Substack, not a product. No event density, no cards, no distribution engine.
- Volume is issues, not a Saturday slate.
- No X presence found.

## Implications for Pundits.Pro

- Ignore unless StreetCred ships a real database or CFB/NFL weekly product.
- Do not spend roadmap on manifesto positioning. The event card is the argument.

## Partnership thesis

At most a methodology or cross-link conversation. No supplier or distribution case today.

## Monitoring checklist

- New sports or a structured database.
- Subscriber/paywall signals on Substack.
- An X account.
- Whether weekly CFB/NFL picks appear.

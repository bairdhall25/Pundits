# Product brief

Status: Canonical

## Product promise

Sports media has abundant predictions and weak memory. Pundits.Pro creates a durable public record: who made the call, what they actually said, what the market believed at the time, and whether the call ultimately hit.

The product is not a sportsbook, tout service, prediction model, or general transcript archive. It is a sports-media accountability and discovery product built from verifiable public picks.

## Primary users

### Sports fan

Wants to know who credible personalities are taking, where experts disagree, and who earned the right to be trusted or faded.

### Search visitor

Arrives with a specific question: who picked this game, what did a pundit predict, what is a pundit's record, or who correctly called a past result?

### Social viewer

Encounters a single high-context artifact: a bold call, disagreement, upset receipt, hit, miss, or updated record. The artifact must make sense without prior product knowledge.

### Operator/editor

Needs a disciplined workflow for finding, verifying, mapping, freezing, grading, publishing, correcting, and recapping claims without corrupting the ledger.

## Core jobs

- Before an event: help a fan see who picked each side and how bold the call was relative to the frozen market.
- During an event: make genuinely live picks easy to follow without mislabeling long-range pending claims as live.
- After an event: show the receipt, result, and updated record quickly.
- Over time: make a pundit's history searchable, comparable, and difficult to rewrite.

## Differentiated value

The value is the combination, not any single component:

1. A recognizable named person.
2. A verified first-person quote.
3. A durable source link and date.
4. A clearly mapped event and side.
5. A frozen market price that conveys difficulty and consensus at capture time.
6. An objective result and visible record after settlement.
7. Permanent pages and share artifacts that compound over time.

Raw scraped assertions are easy to manufacture. A trusted corpus of correctly attributed, objectively mapped, consistently graded picks is harder.

## Current strategy

The product needs sufficient qualified event density and repeated audience behavior before meaningful monetization experiments. Near-term work should prioritize:

- More high-quality picks on events fans already care about.
- Two-sided disagreements and recognizable personalities.
- Fast, defensible grading and corrections.
- Permanent search inventory.
- Shareable receipts and recaps.
- Repeatable editorial operations with measurable latency.

Monetization should follow evidence of attention or utility. Plausible later paths include sponsorships, relevant affiliates, premium history/analytics, data licensing, media widgets, and API access. None should distort capture quality or make the product look like a betting tout.

## Moat hypothesis

The defensible asset is not page count or a complicated score. It is a compounding evidence graph connecting people, exact statements, sources, events, frozen probabilities, outcomes, teams, weeks, and records—combined with a brand fans trust to grade fairly.

Distribution is part of the moat. The data must repeatedly become timely stories, receipts, rankings, and arguments people want to search for and share.

## What success looks like at this stage

- Fans understand the product without learning market jargon.
- A material share of tracked events has recognizable picks, preferably on both sides.
- Settlement-to-grade and grade-to-recap times are consistently short.
- Every mapped pick produces a valid permanent story and social artifact.
- Records reconcile across event, pundit, leaderboard, archive, and feed surfaces.
- Search impressions, social engagement, detail opens, shares, and return visits begin to compound.
- Event coverage grows without a decline in verification or mapping quality.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Treating generic reporting, schedules, contract facts, or vague analysis as predictions.
- Building infrastructure, scoring systems, or monetization tiers ahead of audience need.
- Calling old or season-long pending claims `live` or `in play`.
- Padding empty sides with weak evidence.
- Optimizing raw URL count while allowing thin or inconsistent pages.
- Treating a tiny or selectively captured win-loss record as proof of forecasting skill.
- Losing the source receipt while simplifying the interface.
- Letting automation publish errors faster than humans can audit them.

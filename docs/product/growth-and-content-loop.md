# Growth and content loop

Status: Canonical

## Thesis

Pundits.Pro should build a dense, trustworthy corpus and repeatable distribution engine before optimizing monetization. Every verified pick should create several useful artifacts; every grade should refresh those artifacts and create a new reason to return.

The loop is:

> Capture → verify → map → freeze → publish → distribute → event settles → grade → recap → records improve → search/social discovery → more attention and sources

## Qualified event density

The useful unit is not a scraped claim or page. It is a verified mapped pick with a recognizable speaker, explicit event/side, source receipt, frozen market context, and objective grading path.

Growth targets should track concentration, quantity, and quality:

- Verified mapped picks added.
- Share with named first-person attribution.
- Share with resolvable events inside 7, 30, and 180 days.
- Events with at least one pick.
- Events with picks on both sides.
- Picks from recognizable or high-engagement voices.
- Source-to-capture latency.
- Settlement-to-grade latency.
- Grade-to-recap latency.
- Corrections and disputed mappings.

## Artifact inventory per mapped pick

A mapped pick can directly or indirectly update:

1. The canonical call record.
2. An individual pick-story URL.
3. The event page.
4. The pundit profile.
5. The relevant team page or pages.
6. A sport slate.
7. A permanent weekly archive for game picks.
8. The Takes feed.
9. The compact Book ledger.
10. Leaderboard/activity state.
11. XML/RSS/news feeds and sitemap discovery.
12. Search metadata and structured data.
13. An event social card.
14. A take social card.
15. Vertical story artwork.
16. One or more editorial social posts.

Not every item is a unique indexable URL, and not every artifact should be posted automatically. The value comes from consistent reuse of one evidence-backed object, not synthetic content multiplication.

## Additional artifacts when a pick grades

- Updated hit/miss state on the pick story, event page, pundit profile, leaderboard, team page, and archive.
- Outcome-aware title and description.
- A result receipt card.
- A hit, miss, upset, or fade/follow social post.
- A weekly or event recap.
- Updated pundit record and disagreement outcome.
- Internal links from current coverage to the durable historical result.

Grading is therefore a second publishing event, not maintenance work.

## Editorial packaging

The highest-value packages are likely:

- `Tonight`: picks resolving soon.
- `Live now`: only picks whose games are underway.
- `Biggest disagreements`: recognizable voices on opposing sides.
- `Bold calls`: correct or pending underdog picks at low frozen probabilities.
- `Receipts`: newly graded hits and misses.
- `Who called it`: post-event search and social package.
- `Pundit record`: updated history after a meaningful result.
- `Week N expert picks/results`: durable weekly search pages.

The homepage should be curated from these packages, not merely sorted from the database.

## SEO strategy

The durable search graph connects:

- Pundit × event pick stories.
- Event aggregation pages.
- Pundit history pages.
- Team pages.
- Sport/week archives.
- Result-aware updates after settlement.

Protect permanence. A graded URL becomes more valuable over time because it can answer both pre-event and historical queries. Do not overwrite one season with another or remove old weeks as the schedule advances.

Avoid thin-page inflation. A roster shell or team with no calls should remain non-indexable. A page should exist because it answers a concrete question with source-backed data.

## Social strategy

Every social asset should work without site context and communicate:

- Face and name.
- Exact or faithful short call.
- Event and picked side.
- Frozen probability when it adds meaning.
- Pending, live, hit, or miss state.
- Pundits.Pro identity and a permanent destination URL.

The best social moments are conflict and resolution, not database updates. Prioritize disagreements, bold calls, recognizable people, impending events, and receipts.

## Distribution experiments before monetization

- Ask smaller pundits to verify and share their profile or pick receipt.
- Publish event/weekly recaps quickly enough to join the conversation.
- Test which artifact types generate profile visits, event-detail opens, shares, and returns.
- Build internal linking around teams, weeks, events, and people.
- Measure search impressions and indexing by page type.
- Track organic mentions and backlinks from the pundits or outlets being covered.

## Monetization gate

Do not optimize pricing until the product demonstrates a repeatable attention or utility signal. A monetization experiment should identify the customer, the recurring job, and the evidence that the product already performs that job.

Potential paths remain hypotheses:

- Sponsorship around weekly packages or records.
- Carefully matched affiliate links that do not compromise editorial trust.
- Premium history, alerts, comparison, or deeper analytics.
- Data/API licensing to media, creators, and research products.
- Embeddable widgets or syndicated accountability modules.
- Partnerships with complementary ingestion or media-distribution products.

The corpus and distribution engine create option value. They should not be distorted to force revenue before fans care.
